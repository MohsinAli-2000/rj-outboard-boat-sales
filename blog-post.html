Choosing the Perfect
Outboard Propeller Pitch

Published on March 12, 2026 • 8 Min Read

Outboard motor propeller choice is the single most critical factor in achieving your boat's theoretical top speed, fuel efficiency, and holeshot acceleration. Yet, over 60% of recreational boaters operate with an incorrect pitch.

In our Mississippi delta shop, we see it daily: a brand-new Skeeter or Xpress rigged with a top-of-the-line Yamaha VMAX, but struggling to reach plane under a full load of tackle and livewell water. The culprit is almost always propeller pitch. Put simply, propeller pitch is the theoretical distance (in inches) that a propeller would move forward through a solid medium in one complete rotation. Let's break down how to dial yours in correctly.

1. Understanding Wide Open Throttle (WOT)

Your outboard engine has a specified Wide Open Throttle RPM operating range, typically between 5,000 and 6,000 RPM. To determine if your current prop pitch is correct, run your boat with a normal load of fuel, gear, and passengers, and open the throttle wide.

TECH TIP: THE 200 RPM RULE

A general rule of thumb is that one inch of propeller pitch changes your outboard motor's wide-open throttle RPM by approximately 150 to 200 RPM. Decreasing propeller pitch increases wide-open throttle RPM, whereas increasing pitch decreases wide-open throttle RPM.

2. Aluminum vs. Stainless Steel Props

Aluminum props are economical, easy to repair, and safer if you frequently strike underwater hazards like delta mudbanks or submerged timber. However, they flex under load. Stainless steel propellers are significantly stronger and thinner, suffering almost zero blade flex. This allows for sharper cup profiles and dramatically improved high-speed hookup.

Summary: Finding Your Sweet Spot

Before upgrading, write down your boat's current prop diameter and pitch (stamped directly on the hub), your current max RPM, and your average load. Bring these numbers by RJ's Outboard Sales & Service, and our certified Yamaha mechanics can guide you to the perfect wheel.

Need Expert Rigging Advice?

Stop guessing your pitch. Bring your vessel to RJ's service bay, and we will perform an on-water tachometer test to select the perfect prop.
